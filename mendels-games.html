Named for Mendel

Mendel's Games is named for Mendel, a dog who's the app's first tester.

Set it up, then play along

  1. Pick a game. Fetch-style chases, or treat-finding puzzles.
  2. Pick a tier: Puppy, Adult or Senior. [PLACEHOLDER: what each tier changes, from the spec]
  3. Let your dog play. A nose tap counts as a catch. The tap zones are large, and bigger still on iPad.
  4. Five minutes of play. Then a gentle 10-minute settle-down animation winds play time down.

Toys your dog knows

  • The toys: a ball, a bone, a frisbee, a toy mouse, and a feather with a silver bell.
  • The sounds: a squeaker plays on a catch, and a bell dings. Mute either one whenever you like.
  • Holiday themes for the seasons. [PLACEHOLDER: which holiday themes ship in 1.0, and whether they're free or a purchase]

Made for dog eyes. Dogs have two kinds of color-sensing cones instead of our three, so they tell blues and yellows apart much more easily than reds and greens. That's why every toy is white or yellow on deep navy. (Source: Neitz, Geist and Jacobs, "Color vision in the dog," Visual Neuroscience, 1989.)

Play it safe

  • Stay with your dog. Mendel's Games is for play time with you nearby, not a sitter. Stop if your dog seems frustrated or loses interest.
  • Protect your screen. Use a sturdy case and a tempered-glass screen protector, keep the device steady and out of chewing range, and wipe off nose prints afterwards.
  • Unplug the charger during play, so there's no cord to chew.
  • End with the real thing. A screen toy can't be caught for real, so finish with a real toy or a treat.
